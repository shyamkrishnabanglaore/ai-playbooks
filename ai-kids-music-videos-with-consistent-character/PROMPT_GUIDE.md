# Prompt Guide — Character-Locked 3D Kids Music Video

Use this guide to create the reference, storyboard, and VideoExpress prompts. Keep the character identity stable while allowing scenes to change.

## 1. Freeze identity once

Write a short character bible with stable traits only. Example:

> Pip is a small young red panda with a round russet face, cream muzzle and eye patches, bright brown eyes, soft striped tail, and short tufted ears. Pip always wears the same sunny-yellow raincoat, teal scarf, and green rubber boots, with a tiny blue backpack. Appealing proportions and polished 3D family-feature animation, soft tactile fur, expressive eyes.

The example is illustrative; derive the actual bible from the user's character choice. Repeat it verbatim. Change scene action, framing, and emotion around it, never the identity block.

## 2. Reference image template

`[FULL CHARACTER BIBLE]. One character only, standing in a relaxed neutral pose, front three-quarter view, full character visible from head to feet, face and signature clothing clear, gentle friendly expression, simple uncluttered warm-neutral background, soft even studio lighting, clean landscape 16:9 composition, polished 3D family-animation render.`

Use a neutral, legible reference. It establishes identity; it should not contain a complicated action, extra cast, or obscuring prop.

## 3. Storyboard still template

`[FULL CHARACTER BIBLE]. In [specific setting and time], [one clear action tied to this lyric or musical phrase]. Pip feels [emotion], shown through [specific eyes, brows, head angle, and posture]. [Camera distance and angle]; [important prop size, position, and support]. [Lighting and a few palette cues]. Polished 3D family-feature animation, appealing rounded forms, tactile materials, expressive eyes, colorful storybook production design. Landscape 16:9; keep the face, full outfit, and action readable.`

Use the character's name in scene details but keep the full bible in the prompt. Keep one main action per image. Specify hand roles and object support when props are held. Don't ask a character to use the same hand for multiple incompatible gestures.

## 4. Image-to-video template

`[FULL CHARACTER BIBLE]. Start from [the exact still-image pose and framing]. Over the clip, [one small, continuous action that follows from the pose]. [Subtle eye/head/body movement and gentle environmental motion]. Keep the same character, face, fur/skin, clothing, colors, and proportions throughout. Preserve the landscape 16:9 composition. Keep the camera [steady / a very gentle specified move]. No cuts, identity changes, costume changes, or unsupported object motion.`

Make movement compatible with the still. If a hand is holding something, keep the grip and support believable. Prefer small movement that animates the song beat without changing the character silhouette or hiding the face.

## 5. Consistency checklist

- Character bible is copied exactly and appears before scene-specific details.
- Species/person type, age, face, colors, outfit, and signature accessory remain fixed.
- One scene action is clearly visible and maps to the selected lyric or music phrase.
- The frame remains landscape 16:9 with the hero readable.
- Pose, prop support, camera, and movement directions are physically compatible.
- Motion stays modest enough to preserve identity across a short clip.
- No dialogue or lip-sync instruction is present unless the user approved a singing-on-screen plan.
